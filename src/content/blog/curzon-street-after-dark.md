By [Henry Ashcroft](/about-the-editor), Mayfair Area Specialist

*Last updated: 20 July 2026*

If Berkeley Square is Mayfair's grand set-piece and Shepherd Market its hidden village, Curzon Street is the quiet spine that connects the district and holds a surprising amount of its character. It runs east to west through the heart of Mayfair, and after the shops and offices close it becomes one of the more rewarding evening streets in W1, for one reason above all: it is built around a proper cinema, which gives a Curzon Street evening a shape that most of Mayfair lacks. Here is how the street works after dark, and how to build an evening along it, as of July 2026.

## The Street That Runs Through Mayfair

Curzon Street takes its name from the Curzon family, who held land here as Mayfair was laid out in the eighteenth century, and it has kept an air of quiet consequence ever since. It is not a shopping parade or a restaurant row; it is a residential and institutional street of Georgian and later townhouses, grand doorways and the occasional mansion, running its length between the edge of Berkeley Square and the approach to Park Lane. The grandest survivor is Crewe House, a rare free-standing Mayfair mansion set back behind its own forecourt, now in diplomatic use. Walking it after dark, you get the thing Mayfair does best and advertises least: a handsome, lamplit street with almost nobody on it.

## An Evening at the Cinema

The street's evening anchor is the Curzon Mayfair, the storied art-house cinema that has stood on Curzon Street since the 1930s and remains one of the most civilised places to watch a film in London. This is what gives a Curzon Street night its backbone: a proper cultural set-piece you can build an evening around, rather than just a row of bars. From experience, a film here is one of the great underrated Mayfair evenings, an early screening followed by a walk and a drink, and it turns a night on this street into something with a centre. Few parts of the West End let you fold a genuine cinema into an elegant evening this easily, and it is the single best reason to point yourself at Curzon Street after dark.

## Character in the Details

Curzon Street rewards the walker who looks. This is the street of Heywood Hill, the famous old bookshop with its literary history, and of long-established institutions that give the pavement a texture the newer parts of Mayfair cannot fake. The shopfronts are lit softly after hours, the residential stretches go quiet and grand, and the whole street has the feel of a place that has seen a great deal and mentions none of it. I noticed years ago that Curzon Street is where you send someone who thinks they have seen Mayfair: it is the district with the volume turned down, all character and no clamour, and it is at its best in the hour after the working day drains out of it.

## The Walk into Shepherd Market

Curzon Street's best trick is where it takes you. Halfway along, a set of covered passages and side lanes lead south into [Shepherd Market](/blog/shepherd-market-after-dark), the little village of pubs and lanes that is one of Mayfair's loveliest evening pockets, and the two work as a pair: the grand, quiet street and the tucked-away village a minute apart. This is the walk I recommend most on this side of Mayfair, because it gives an evening two completely different moods within a few hundred metres. The bars and restaurants thin along Curzon Street itself and cluster in the market and the streets around it, so treat the street as the handsome approach and Shepherd Market as where the evening lands, with the grander [Mayfair hotel bars](/blog/mayfair-hotel-bars) a short walk further for a serious nightcap.

## Building the Evening

Here is the shape I recommend, having walked this street at every hour. Start with an early screening at the cinema, or simply a dusk walk along the street for its architecture and its quiet. Drift south through the passages into Shepherd Market for a drink among the pubs and the lanes, where the pace picks up and the evening finds its people. Eat there or nearby, keeping dining to whatever suits the night. Then finish in one of two directions: north and west toward the hotel bars for a grand last drink, or a gentle wind-down in the market itself. The whole route is a short walk, entirely in the oldest part of Mayfair, and it gives you culture, quiet and conviviality in one evening, which is more than most London streets manage. The wider Mayfair drinking map, which [Time Out's bars and pubs guide](https://www.timeout.com/london/bars-and-pubs) tracks across the city, has louder streets, but few with this range on a single walk.

## When to Go

Curzon Street is at its best on a weekday evening, when the cinema is running, the street is quiet and Shepherd Market is lively without being crushed. A film-led evening works any night the programme suits you, which is part of the appeal. Fridays bring more life to the market end; weekends are quieter on the street itself, since this is a working-week and residential address, which makes a Saturday early-evening stroll unexpectedly peaceful. In winter the lamplit townhouses and the glow of the cinema front give the street a particular warmth, and the early dark only helps, as of July 2026.

### Q: What is Curzon Street known for?

**A:** Its cinema above all: the Curzon Mayfair, an art-house institution on the street since the 1930s. Beyond that it is one of Mayfair's grand, quiet residential streets, home to the old Heywood Hill bookshop and the mansion Crewe House, running through the heart of the district.

### Q: Is Curzon Street worth visiting at night?

**A:** Yes, for a particular kind of evening. It is quiet and handsome rather than lively, and its real value is as the approach to Shepherd Market and as the home of a proper cinema, which lets you build a cultural evening most of Mayfair cannot offer.

### Q: How do you get from Curzon Street to Shepherd Market?

**A:** Through the covered passages and side lanes that lead south off Curzon Street; the two sit a minute apart. It is one of the nicest short walks in Mayfair, moving from a grand quiet street into a tucked-away village of pubs in a few hundred metres.

### Q: Is Curzon Street a good place for a night out?

**A:** As part of a walk rather than a destination in itself. The street is elegant and calm; the bars and restaurants cluster in and around Shepherd Market at one end and toward the hotel bars at the other, so use Curzon Street as the spine and let the evening happen off it, as of July 2026.

Curzon Street after dark is Mayfair with its coat still on: grand, quiet, literary, and anchored by one of the best small cinemas in London. Walk it at dusk, fold in a film or a drink, and let it carry you down into Shepherd Market for the rest of the night.
